Teaching & Learning

Use AI to design for learning, not just to produce content

AI can draft a syllabus, a quiz or a lesson in seconds. The hard part is making sure the goals, the assessments and the class activities fit together so students actually learn. This page shows faculty where AI helps with each part, and gives them tools to try it on their own course today.

Leading AI at your institution? Share this page with faculty, instructional designers and teaching-center staff. No AI experience needed.

The framework: align, then improve

A well-designed course keeps three things aligned: what students should learn, how you'll know they learned it, and how they practice. Then it uses evidence to keep getting better.

Interactive: click each part of the model to see where AI helps

Grounded in backward design (Wiggins & McTighe), constructive alignment (Biggs) and the learning engineering approach behind CMU's Open Learning Initiative.

Try it on a real task

The AI for Learning Companion is a chatbot built on this framework. Pick the situation that sounds most like yours, copy the starter prompt, and paste it into the companion.

1Copy the prompt2Open the companion3Paste, then fill in the [brackets] with your course

01 · Outcomes

“My syllabus says students will ‘understand’ the material. How would I even know if they do?”

Starter prompt

I teach [course] to [who your students are]. Here are my current learning objectives: [paste them].
Rewrite each one so it names something students can do that I could see or assess. For each, tell me what student work would show they've met it.

You'll get objectives you can actually assess, and a first idea of how to assess each one.

Open the companion ↗
02 · Assessment

“Students can hand in a polished essay in minutes with AI. I can't tell what they actually learned.”

Starter prompt

Here is an assignment I give: [paste it]. Its goal is for students to [what they should be able to do].
If a student uses AI on it, what would the result still show about their learning, and what wouldn't it? Suggest one revision that keeps the goal but asks students to show their own thinking.

You'll get an honest check on the assignment and one revision you could use this term.

Open the companion ↗
03 · Instruction

“Students do the reading, but on the test they still can't apply the idea to a new problem.”

Starter prompt

My students read about [topic] but can't apply it to new problems. I want them to be able to [goal].
Design a 15-minute class activity: one worked example that shows each step, one practice problem for students to try, and feedback for the most common mistake.

You'll get a short activity that gives students practice, not just more to read.
