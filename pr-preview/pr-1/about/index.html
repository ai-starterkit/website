About the AI Starter Kit

A collective effort to give broad-access institutions practical AI tools and guidance, shaped by the institutions that will use them.

Why it exists

Broad-access institutions are under increasing pressure to improve student outcomes, strengthen workforce alignment, and operate efficiently with limited resources, and AI is beginning to reshape how they work. But advancing AI adoption isn’t primarily a tooling challenge. It’s a capacity and infrastructure challenge. The AI Starter Kit builds the connective layer that helps institutions learn from one another, reduce duplication, and take a practical next step.

How we work

Built with institutionsPeople from HBCUs, community colleges and universities shape each tool and help test it.
Curated what’s workingFor each area, we point to a few vetted resources, with a note on what each is best for.
Build only what's missingIf a tool already exists, we don’t recreate it. We only build where there's a real gap.
Open to adaptEverything we build is openly licensed as a public good, so your campus can adapt it to fit.

How it started

  1. Summer 2025

    The idea starts at UNITE in Atlanta: an "AI in a Box" to help colleges figure out how AI can work for them.

  2. Fall 2025

    Conversations with institutions, partner organizations, and funders build support for the idea and lead to the first grants.

  3. Spring 2026

    Partner organizations come together to plan the AI Starter Kit.

  4. July 2026

    The AI Starter Kit officially kicks off with a workshop at North Carolina Central University in Durham, where partners mapped priorities for the work ahead.

  5. Fall 2026

    Working groups take on maturity assessment, policy, technology architecture, and teaching and learning innovation. The first version of this site goes live.

  6. Next

    More tools, use-cases, and resources, shaped by what broad-access institutions need most. If something would help your institution, we'd love to hear about it.

Who's building the AI Starter Kit

About 60 leaders from HBCUs, community colleges, universities, and partner organizations met in Durham in July to work on the AI Starter Kit together. Working groups have since picked up each part.
