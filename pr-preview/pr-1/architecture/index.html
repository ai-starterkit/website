Technology Architecture

What enterprise AI stacks could look like for broad-access institutions, such as community colleges and HBCUs, with templates and tools that make them attainable for institutions with limited technical capacity.

Architecture Bot

Developed with Ed Advancement and a working group of campus technology leaders

In the near term, the working group is defining the reference materials, intake questions and decision criteria behind an advisory bot that helps institutional IT teams translate AI use cases into candidate early architectures. It will help institutions in the AI Starter Kit Implementation Pilot gain experience building and deploying AI solutions.

Step 1 · Describe

The problems you want to solve with AI, and your current environment: diagrams, documentation or a few quick questions.

Step 2 · Map capabilities

The bot translates your use cases into the capabilities you need, described independently of any product or vendor.

Step 3 · Compare options

Several candidate architectures, each with the capabilities it covers, its tradeoffs and the criteria that would favor it.

Each option the bot suggests includes:

  • A short summary
  • A diagram of the main components and how data moves between them
  • How it works and connects to your current systems
  • Which capabilities it covers, and which it leaves open
  • Tradeoffs: cost, staffing and skills, time to value, vendor lock-in, privacy and security, and accuracy risk
  • When an institution should choose it

Early stage

Toward an open education AI stack

Over time, the working group plans to extend this work into a fuller toolkit for designing and adopting a long-term AI stack. That likely means finding gaps in today’s market and filling them with open-source components that institutions can run in their own environments.

The group is taking stock of existing efforts in this space and will set next steps after the October 27 convening.
