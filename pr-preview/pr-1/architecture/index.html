Technology Architecture

What enterprise AI stacks could look like for broad-access institutions, such as community colleges and HBCUs, with templates and tools that make them attainable for institutions with limited technical capacity.

Most institutions already pay for systems with AI built in, and new tools arrive constantly. Planning your architecture first helps you choose what fits your problems, budget and team, and avoid being locked in to one vendor.

What should I do first?

  • Name two or three problems you want AI to help solve, such as slow answers to common student questions or hours spent on manual data entry.
  • List what you run today, including AI features already built into products you use, and gather any diagrams or documentation.
  • Request access to the Architecture Bot and describe your problems and environment.
  • Review the options it suggests with your IT team and the people who will use the solution.

Architecture Bot

Developed with Ed Advancement and a working group of campus technology leaders

The Architecture Bot helps institutional IT teams turn AI use cases into candidate early architectures. A working version is being tested now, and an improved version will be ready for institutions in the AI Starter Kit Implementation Pilot in early 2027.

Step 1 · Describe

The problems you want to solve with AI, and your current environment: diagrams, documentation or a few quick questions.

Step 2 · Map capabilities

The bot translates your use cases into the capabilities you need, described independently of any product or vendor.

Step 3 · Compare options

Several candidate architectures, each with the capabilities it covers, its tradeoffs and the criteria that would favor it.

Each option the bot suggests includes:

  • A short summary
  • A diagram of the main components and how data moves between them
  • How it works and connects to your current systems
  • Which capabilities it covers, and which it leaves open
  • Tradeoffs: cost, staffing and skills, time to value, vendor lock-in, privacy and security, and accuracy risk
  • When an institution should choose it

Can I trust this?

The bot describes the capabilities you need before it names any product, and every option lists its tradeoffs: cost, staffing, vendor lock-in, privacy and accuracy risk. Ed Advancement develops it with a working group of campus technology leaders who test each version. Its suggestions are a starting point for your IT team's own planning.

Who has already done it?

The working group is collecting reference architectures from institutions and will add them here once reviewed. Has your institution built an AI architecture others could learn from? Tell us.

Early stage

Toward an open education AI stack

Over time, the working group plans to extend this work into a fuller toolkit for designing and adopting a long-term AI stack. That likely means finding gaps in today’s market and filling them with open-source components that institutions can run in their own environments.

The group is taking stock of existing efforts in this space and agreeing who will lead each part of the work.
