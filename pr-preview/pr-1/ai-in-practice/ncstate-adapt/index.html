Institutional Use Case Collection

North Carolina State University

ADAPT: University-Wide Data Science & AI Education

  • Stage: 3-5 Piloting & Capability Building / Institutional Integration & Scaling
  • Scope: Curriculum Development and Workforce Alignment
  • Scale: Institution-wide deployment

What it is

NC State's Data Science and AI Academy was a university-wide initiative that made data science and AI education accessible to students from any major, built around the ADAPT model: project-based teaching with 10 common learning elements and a workforce development focus. The model let instructors from many disciplines and course topics deliver consistent courses and join a shared professional learning community with twice-monthly meetings.

Problem addressed

Students across all disciplines lacked access to data science and AI skills, and no consistent institutional approach existed for building that capacity. The initiative also aimed to extend upskilling to faculty and staff.

How it was built

The initiative originated from a working group convened to address institutional needs in data science and AI. Its leader proposed it, and the Provost's office funded it with the Office of Research and Innovation. Governance was both top-down (a Provost-initiated all-campus unit) and bottom-up (faculty-led course and credential development). Instructors integrated AI course-by-course, some focusing on AI skills directly and others on applying AI in student workflows. The Provost's office supported business operations and HR, while programmes used existing computing infrastructure.

Cost & resources

The programme cost approximately $1.5M per year, covering 7 full-time staff (Executive Director, Programs Specialist, Communications Specialist, Grants Officer, Director of Academic Programs, K-12 Programs Specialist, and Executive Assistant), affiliated faculty, teaching assistants, graduate and undergraduate student effort, and facilities. Additional funding came from federal and foundation grants, including 2 NSF grants.

Outcomes

The programme scaled from 5 to 40 ADAPT courses over five years, reaching students across all 12 colleges and over 173 majors. ADAPT courses were articulated into majors, concentrations, minors, and certificates across campus, with 6 minors and 2 certificates developed in collaboration with departments. Non-credit contracts were established with the NC Department of Health and Human Services and with Ablr, an organisation focused on AI literacy for low vision and blind individuals.

Risks

The primary risk identified was that demand for courses would exceed capacity. The institution noted this was a funding constraint rather than a staffing one, as a waitlist of qualified instructors from industry, government, and other academic institutions already existed.
