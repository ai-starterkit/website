Institutional Use Case Collection

EDHEC Business School

Virtual Teaching Assistant: Course Q&A Chatbot

  • Stage: 3-5 Piloting & Capability Building / Institutional Integration & Scaling
  • Scope: Teaching, Learning, and Assessment Strategies
  • Scale: Institution-wide service

What it is

EDHEC developed an AI chatbot embedded in its Blackboard LMS through a partnership with Amanote, designed to answer student questions using only instructor-uploaded course materials. The system used a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) approach powered by Amazon Bedrock and Claude Sonnet, enabling contextual Q&A based exclusively on content the instructor had approved.

Problem addressed

The institution needed to support over 10,000 students with heterogeneous needs and offer 24/7 academic support that faculty could not provide at scale. It also sought to find ways to engage students more actively with course materials while integrating AI in a way that preserved academic integrity.

How it was built

The solution was built on Amanote, a note-taking tool that had already been embedded in EDHEC's LMS for several years and was widely adopted by students. This made the transition to an AI assistant a natural extension rather than a disruptive change. The project combined top-down strategic direction from institutional leadership and the Centre for Teaching and Learning (CTL) with close co-creation between pedagogical experts, faculty volunteers, and the Amanote team. Instructors uploaded course materials, the AI processed them, and students interacted with the assistant during learning sessions. Responses were continuously evaluated through student feedback to enable iterative improvement.

Cost & resources

The project was developed with AWS support. Resources included the Amanote tech team, AWS cloud infrastructure, and EDHEC's PILab pedagogical team. No specific cost figure was provided.

Outcomes

The pilot demonstrated strong adoption, with students asking thousands of questions. Usage peaked during exam periods, confirming the tool's value as a study companion. At the time of submission, the institution was exploring how the tool could be adapted to assist staff with document management.

Risks

The main risks were AI inaccuracies and student over-reliance. The RAG architecture mitigated both by drawing exclusively on faculty-uploaded materials rather than a general knowledge base, with all answers traceable to source. Faculty materials were never reused by external systems, and student data stayed within Amanote's secure infrastructure in line with privacy standards.
