Institutional Use Case Collection

Boise State University

Cross-Campus AI Institute for Faculty (1.0 & 2.0)

  • Stage: 3-4 Piloting & Capability Building / Transition & Investment Planning
  • Scope: Faculty Professional Development
  • Scale: Institution-wide programme

What it is

Boise State ran a recurring three-day faculty development institute on AI literacy across all disciplines, structured around the DEC AI Literacy Framework. Designed as a shame-free, active-learning environment, it held a roughly 50/50 split between direct instruction and hands-on work. Each session carried a defined learning outcome and a concrete deliverable, delivered in hybrid format to in-person and online participants.

Problem addressed

Faculty AI adoption was uneven and largely driven by STEM and business units, leaving other disciplines without shared language, practical tools, or a safe space to engage. Two campus AI strategy summits identified this gap, and the institutes responded directly to that finding.

How it was built

The School for the Digital Future hosted the institute with the College of Business and Economics, the Centre for Teaching and Learning, and eCampus. It combined bottom-up collaboration with top-down funding and approval. The DEC framework guided planning and delivery, with sessions progressing from Level 1 to Level 3 across the programme. Institute 2.0 embedded institutional AI tool guidance (boisestate.ai and a Gemini licence with workspace options) into the training and added five campus partners. All sessions were recorded.

Cost & resources

Costs included $1,000 stipends for each of the 10 faculty leads and $250 stipends for each participant, plus a standard event budget for a three-day institute (8:30am–3:30pm) covering lunch and space rental. Top-down funding approval and physical infrastructure were required.

Outcomes

Institute 1.0 (May 2025) served 61 participants from 10 campus units, ranging from non-adopters to advanced adopters, and produced statistically significant improvements across all five UNESCO AI competency areas. 78% of participants established new inter-professional connections, and the environment was noted for being inclusive and shame-free. An internal white paper was produced from the findings. Institute 2.0 (May 2026) grew to approximately 90 participants plus the planning team, with representation from every college on campus. Preliminary satisfaction data was strong. A certificate and micro-credential pathway was under development using the recorded sessions.

Risks

The only identified risk was low attendance or the institute failing to feel meaningful. May 2026 data showed strong satisfaction.
