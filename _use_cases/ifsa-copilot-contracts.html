Institute for Study Abroad

Copilot-Assisted Review of Contracts & MOUs

  • Stage: 3 Piloting & Capability Building
  • Scope: Operational Readiness
  • Scale: Single workflow pilot

What it is

The Institute for Study Abroad developed a structured process using Microsoft Copilot (enterprise version) to support staff in reviewing external service agreements, housing contracts, and MOUs against a best-practice framework. Copilot was used to extract key clauses and obligations, compare agreements against the framework and prior agreements, and flag deviations, omissions, or unusual terms. Outputs were reviewed by staff; Copilot did not replace human judgement.

Problem addressed

The institution faced the challenge of reviewing highly varied service, housing, and MOU agreements in a reliable and efficient way. Each agreement was distinctive in structure, terminology, and profile, making it difficult to benchmark manually. Reviews had been dependent on individual staff knowledge and memory, creating risk and inconsistency.

How it was built

Staff reviewed multiple historical agreements to develop a best-practice framework. Using Copilot, this framework was converted into a reference document and a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP). A ready-to-use prompt was created to support consistent application of the framework across teams. Copilot was layered on top of existing Microsoft 365 enterprise licensing, requiring no new infrastructure. All agreements continued to follow established institutional approval and signature processes.

Cost & resources

There was no additional cost. The initiative was implemented entirely on existing Microsoft 365 Copilot enterprise licensing. The only resource required was staff time to curate example agreements, define best-practice benchmarks, and develop SOPs and guidance documents.

Outcomes

The initiative created a shared, institution-wide benchmark for reviewing agreements. It improved consistency and confidence in contract reviews; reduced reliance on individual memory and experience; increased productivity through faster initial reviews; improved risk documentation quality; and gave staff greater empowerment in partner negotiations. Earlier clarity with partners also helped reduce late-stage revisions and delays.

Risks

Identified risks included over-reliance on AI-generated summaries, data sensitivity when handling contractual documents, and inconsistent use of the framework across teams. These were mitigated through clear guidance that Copilot supported but did not replace human review, use restricted to approved Microsoft 365 environments, and shared SOPs to ensure consistent application.
