Boise State University

AI Literacy Curriculum for Nursing Faculty

  • Stage: 3 Piloting & Capability Building
  • Scope: AI Literacy & Ethical Use
  • Scale: School-level initiative

What it is

Boise State's School of Nursing developed a scaffolded AI literacy curriculum for nursing faculty, built around the DEC AI Literacy Framework with Dimension 5 redefined to reflect nursing-specific competencies. The initiative also produced a faculty-approved set of AI use icons for course assignments, based on the AI Assignment Standards (AIAS) framework. It was driven by the SON AI Task Force, a standing committee established specifically for this purpose.

Problem addressed

Nursing educators faced tension between skill development and academic evaluation, alongside the need to prepare students to interact with AI tools in healthcare practice. Faculty had limited knowledge and confidence in AI, and concerns about inappropriate student AI use were growing, particularly in online programmes. No unit-level guidance existed despite the university having a strong institution-wide AI foundation.

How it was built

The initiative was a grassroots implementation driven by the SON AI Task Force Committee, operating through shared governance via a formal service committee structure. The team first secured unit-level administrative leadership support, then established the Task Force with representation from instructional design, administration, and faculty across programmes. The DEC framework was identified through the AAC&U AI Institute as the basis for the curriculum, and nursing-specific competencies were defined for Dimension 5. The AI use icons were developed and approved through the shared governance process.

Cost & resources

The only direct cost was the $5,000 fee for the AAC&U AI Institute. Indirect costs included staff and faculty workload time. The team consisted of 5 members plus one undergraduate research assistant, and used the existing LMS throughout.

Outcomes

The initiative increased faculty AI literacy, enabling more informed decisions on when and how to engage with AI in courses. It provided greater clarity for students on acceptable AI use in coursework, deepened institutional understanding of how AI was being implemented in healthcare, and prompted more intentional evaluation of assessment design across nursing programmes.

Risks

No risks were identified. The project was framed as a curiosity-based conversation to better understand how AI was impacting nursing education, not a mandate to use AI.
