IE University

Mapping Faculty AI Adoption in Teaching

  • Stage: 2–3 Governance & Strategic Foundations / Piloting & Capability Building
  • Scope: Teaching, Learning, and Assessment Strategies
  • Scale: Institution-Wide Study

What it is

IE University conducted a structured research initiative combining a faculty-wide survey and in-depth interviews of at least 30 minutes each to map how faculty across the institution were adopting and integrating AI into teaching and learning. Analysis used thematic coding and LDA (Latent Dirichlet Allocation) to identify patterns in practices, barriers, and enablers.

Problem addressed

IE University sought to understand how faculty were adopting and integrating AI into teaching and learning, identify emerging best practices, surface barriers and enablers, and detect training needs. The initiative responded to the rapid and uneven adoption of AI across disciplines and the need for evidence-based insights to inform institutional strategy, faculty support, and learning innovation priorities.

How it was built

The initiative was bottom-up, led by the Director of Learning Innovation, with broad faculty participation across schools. A structured survey was distributed to full-time and adjunct faculty, followed by in-depth qualitative interviews conducted in person and online. Faculty used a mix of institutional AI licences and external tools such as ChatGPT Edu and Claude to support their teaching. Thematic and LDA-based analysis was applied to surface patterns across the data.

Cost & resources

Costs were primarily related to faculty and Learning Innovation staff time. There was no technology spend.

Outcomes

The initiative produced a clear map of AI adoption stages and use cases across disciplines, identified four dominant pedagogical AI patterns (AI-Enhanced Assessment and Critical Comparison; AI Governance and Student Agency; Personalisation and Teaching Aid Development; Critical Reflection and Core Skills Preservation), and surfaced specific faculty training needs, particularly around assessment redesign and peer learning. The findings were being used as the foundation for designing targeted training and peer-learning communities.

Risks

The institution identified uneven adoption creating inconsistency across programmes as the main potential risk.
